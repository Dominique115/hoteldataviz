Dominique Chen – Hotel Booking Patterns & Timing

Theme: Monthly booking patterns, arrival timing, and customer segment behavior.
These views explore how hotels receive and fulfill bookings across time, and how different customer and market segments relate to key timing metrics.

Overall Research Question

What are the monthly patterns of hotel booking requests, arrivals, and stay length — and how do customer type and market segment relate to timing across the booking span?

View-specific Analytic Questions

  • View 1: What are monthly patterns of hotel booking requests, arrivals, and stay length? How does this relate to earnings (ADR)?
  • View 2: How do customer type and market segment relate to different timing patterns across the booking span?

Views

View 1 – Monthly Booking, Arrival & Stay Patterns

ADR is calculated by dividing the sum of all lodging transactions by the total number of staying nights.

The monthly booking count charts reveal a clear seasonal rhythm across both hotel types. City hotels sustain relatively higher booking volumes year-round, while resort hotels show sharper seasonal spikes, peaking in the spring months and October, likely preparing for summer and fall vacation periods. Arrival counts forcity hotels maintaining steadier arrivals throughout the year, while resort hotels experience higher numbers during the summer. Average daily rates (ADR) for resort hotel climbs in summer and dips in off-peak months, whereas city hotel ADR remains more stable across the year, reflecting its heavier reliance on business and corporate travelers who are less sensitive to the calendar.

The ADR vs. total nights scatter shows that the majority of stays cluster at shorter durations across a wide ADR range, while extended stays are mostly recorded to be with resort hotels, concentrate at lower-to-mid ADR values. Suggesting that longer stays tend to come with rate discounts, or that budget-conscious guests self-select into multi-night bookings. The booking date vs. arrival date panel reinforces that most guests plan within a few months of arrival, with a small fraction booking very far in advance, likely for peak-season stays that require earlier reservation windows.

View 2 – Customer Type & Market Segment Timing

Customer Type:

  • Contract – Guests under corporate or negotiated-rate contracts.
  • Group – Bookings made for groups (e.g., events, conferences).
  • Transient – Individual, non-group travelers making independent bookings.
  • Transient-Party – Small parties or families traveling together but not large groups.

Market Segment:

  • Aviation Crew – Airline staff with dedicated negotiated contracts.
  • Complimentary – Free stays (e.g., service recovery or compensation).
  • Corporate Clients – Corporate-negotiated bookings.
  • Direct Booking – Booked directly through the hotel (website, phone).
  • Group Reservations – Reservations made under group codes.
  • Offline Tour Operators – Bookings through traditional travel agencies.
  • Online Travel Agents – Online booking platforms (website, apps).

Time Stamps:

  • Days in Waitlist – Time from booking request until confirmed booking.
  • Days before Arrival – Time from confirmed booking to hotel arrival.
  • Days Stayed – Time from arrival to departure.

The timing heatmap shows that waitlist times are negligible for most customer and segment combinations, with group reservations and certain corporate bookings being the exceptions that occasionally require a wait. The days-before-arrival dimension reveals the sharpest differences: contract and many group customers book with much longer lead times than transient travelers, who show a wide variety of preference for booking lead times. This aligns with the nature of those relationships, corporate contracts and group events require advance planning, while individual leisure or business travelers have more flexibility.

Across market segments, online travel agents dominate transient bookings, while offline tour operators are more associated with contract bookings. Days stayed is generally short for groups and contract customers at city hotel. Transient customers show a more varied pattern, but their stays are generally longer. Overall, the visualization suggests that customer type and market segment are closely linked, knowing one helps us to understand the other, and that timing behavior is shaped more by the nature of the stay (planned event vs. independent trip) than by booking channel alone.
